flutter_unified_map_marker_cluster

Marker clustering for flutter_unified_map with the API of flutter_map_marker_cluster 8.2: clusters split and merge with an animation when the zoom level changes, also during a pinch, and a cluster that zooming cannot split spreads its markers around it (spiderfy).

Spiderfy

A cluster that zooming cannot split — markers stacked on the same point, or a cluster still whole at maxZoom — spreads its markers around it on tap. Three layouts:

  • circle — up to circleSpiralSwitchover markers (9 by default) on a circle spiderfyCircleRadius pixels around the cluster;
  • spiral — from that count on, an Archimedean spiral ("snail"), its spacing widened by spiderfySpiralDistanceMultiplier (keep it above 1 so the markers do not overlap);
  • your own — spiderfyShapePositions receives the marker count and the cluster's position in pixels and returns a position per marker.
MarkerClusterLayerOptions(
  circleSpiralSwitchover: 9,            // circle up to 9 markers, spiral from there on
  spiderfyCircleRadius: 40,
  spiderfySpiralDistanceMultiplier: 2,  // a wider snail for dense stacks
  spiderLegs: const SpiderLegOptions(), // native maps only: a leg joins every marker back to the cluster
)

The markers glide out with animationsOptions.spiderfy; a map tap or a zoom change folds them back in. On Android, iOS and macOS the legs are polylines (spiderLegs, null draws none); flutter_map_marker_cluster draws no legs, so web, Windows and Linux never show them.

Migrating from flutter_map_marker_cluster

Import package:flutter_unified_map/flutter_unified_map.dart and package:flutter_unified_map_marker_cluster/flutter_unified_map_marker_cluster.dart instead of flutter_map and flutter_map_marker_cluster. MarkerClusterLayerWidget, MarkerClusterLayerOptions (every option, with the same names, types and defaults), AnimationsOptions, PolygonOptions, MarkerClusterNode and MarkerNode keep their names; Marker and LatLngBounds are flutter_unified_map's.

Differences:

  • Bitmaps. On Android, iOS and macOS the markers are native: every marker child and cluster widget is rasterised once per key. Give markers an iconKey that captures their look (see Marker), and clusters a clusterIconKey when the builder shows more than the count (the default key is the number of markers).
  • No popupOptions (flutter_map_marker_popup): open your popup from onMarkerTap, for example with a MarkerLayer holding a selected pin, or a Flutter overlay.
  • Native gestures. Native markers report single taps only: onMarkerDoubleTap, onMarkerHoverEnter and onMarkerHoverExit are not called there. With markerChildBehavior: true, onMarkerTap and centerMarkerOnClick are skipped as on flutter_map, and Marker.onTap still fires.
  • Curves. Native markers move with the platform's ease-in-out; the AnimationsOptions curves of the split, merge, fade and spiderfy animations apply on flutter_map only. fitBoundCurves and centerMarkerCurves are passed to the camera animations everywhere (the native camera ignores curves, see flutter_unified_map's STATUS).
  • Spiderfy. As in flutter_map_marker_cluster, a tapped cluster spiderfies when zooming cannot split it. Natively:
    • this also covers markers sharing the exact same point (flutter_map_marker_cluster first zooms to maxZoom, then spiderfies on a second tap);
    • the camera centers such a cluster at its current zoom, where flutter_map_marker_cluster rounds the zoom up;
    • the spiderfied cluster stays under its markers at 30 % opacity;
    • legs join the markers to the cluster once they arrived, styled by the extra spiderLegs option (null draws none; flutter_map_marker_cluster draws no legs, so web, Windows and Linux never show them);
    • a map tap (not a long press) or a zoom closes the spider;
    • when the tap first moves the camera to center the cluster, the spider opens once the platform reports the camera there, and not at all if the user takes the camera elsewhere meanwhile;
    • the hull polygon is drawn solid.
  • Callbacks. onClusterTap receives a MarkerClusterNode of this package. On flutter_map it is converted from flutter_map_marker_cluster's node with its subtree (its parent is null). onMarkersClustered receives, natively, the markers shown alone that join a cluster (zooming out, a spider closing).
  • Rebuilds. As in flutter_map_marker_cluster, a new markers list rebuilds the clusters and a new computeSize alone does not; on every platform a new maxClusterRadius, disableClusteringAtZoom, size or alignment rebuilds them too. Natively the tree is kept when the new markers have the same keys and points as the previous ones (only the markers it hands out change), and an equivalent list sends nothing to the map (see below). A real change rebuilds the tree on the UI thread, in O(markers × zoom levels): a few tens of milliseconds for 4000 markers.
  • Marker keys. Give every marker a unique Key: native ids, and so the transitions across list changes, follow the keys; a marker without a key is identified by its index. Duplicate keys fail an assertion in debug builds.
  • Invalid coordinates. A marker with a NaN or infinite coordinate is left out and reported through FlutterError.reportError (flutter_map_marker_cluster throws).

From flutter_unified_map's former MarkerClusterLayer

MarkerClusterLayer(options:) becomes MarkerClusterLayerWidget(options:), with flutter_map_marker_cluster's defaults: disableClusteringAtZoom 20 (clusters up to and including that level), padding is the padding of the camera fit on a cluster tap (default zero) and alignment is nullable. With zoomToBoundsOnClick: false every cluster tap spiderfies unless spiderfyCluster: false: an app that moves the camera itself in onClusterTap must set it, or its camera move closes the spider right after it opened.

How it works on native maps

The tree is built once per marker list, with flutter_map_marker_cluster's distance grid and radius semantics, so the clusters are the same as on flutter_map at the same zoom (the cluster level is the zoom rounded up). The layer publishes the elements of that level in the viewport grown by one viewport on each side (at most 1500: those on screen first, then the nearest to the screen), as one atomic generation through flutter_unified_map's MarkerPublisher:

  • the area is clustered again when the camera settles (unless the last generation still covers it), as soon as the zoom level changes during a gesture or an animation (0.05 past the level's boundary, so that a pinch hovering on an integer zoom does not flip the clusters; a settled camera always shows the exact level), and when a gesture moved the camera by more than a quarter of the viewport (at most every 150 ms);
  • a level change sends a MarkerTransition of animationsOptions.zoom: an element that was not on the map slides out of the element on the map that contains it, an element leaving slides into the new element that contains it, the others fade; elements present before and after keep their id and do not move. Duration.zero turns it off;
  • a spider opening or closing is the same transition with animationsOptions.spiderfy;
  • a cluster tap fits the camera to the cluster with animationsOptions.fitBound, a marker tap centers it with animationsOptions.centerMarker, and the cluster's convex hull is shown while the map zooms in (showPolygon).

Object ids depend on the marker keys: a marker is MapLayerState.objectId(marker.key, index), a cluster '<layerId>/c<zoom>:<id of its first marker without the layer id>'. A new list of equivalent markers therefore gives the same ids: nothing is sent to the map, an open spider stays open, and transitions continue across it. A tap is resolved against the tree of the generation the map shows, which may be older than the newest one while that one waits for its icons.

A generation can reach the map before the previous one finished animating, or together with it (the map merges the generations set before one push). So a new element slides out of where its container is on screen, estimated from the container's own glide (ease-in-out over the transition's duration), and the elements still sliding away are sent on into the new element that contains them: during a fast pinch across several levels everything comes out of, and goes back into, what the user sees.

The area wraps across the antimeridian like the native maps, and distances to the screen are measured the short way round. When the camera settles, the icons of the on-screen clusters of the neighbouring levels are rasterised ahead (at most 48, nearest the center first, four at a time), so a zoom step rarely waits for them.

Example

example/example.dart is a minimal map. The example/ app of the repository opens on the Brussels city network — 120 pins under one cluster: tap it and the camera zooms in while the cluster splits down to every pin — plus a spiderfy page (one stack of pins on the same point — a single cluster that a tap spreads on a spiral); its tune button changes the animation durations, and its end-to-end tests run on the native maps.
